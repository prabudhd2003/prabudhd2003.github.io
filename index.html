01 About

Background, current research, and what I am looking for next.

I am a master's student in Computer Science at the University of Southern California, on the Artificial Intelligence track, advised through coursework and project work in machine learning and applied AI. Before USC I completed a bachelor's degree in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning at GGSIP University in Delhi, graduating with a 9.45/10 aggregate and two peer-reviewed papers.

My research concerns how meaning is distributed across modalities. In natural conversation, the signal that matters is rarely confined to the words: it is carried by prosody, by facial affect, and by timing relative to the other speaker. My current project treats this directly. Given a recorded dyadic exchange, the task is to localize the specific utterance at which the interaction shifts from cooperative to adversarial — a problem that requires aligning three asynchronous streams at utterance granularity rather than pooling them into a single clip-level embedding. A transformer architecture I designed and trained from scratch reaches 86.7% accuracy on this task over twenty hours of annotated dialogue, exceeding a strong Gemini API baseline by 7.2 percentage points.

Running alongside the research is a second interest in whether these methods survive contact with practice. Over two summers at Novartis and ZS Associates I built gradient-boosted models for identifying early adopters of therapies in markets that did not yet exist, and retrieval-augmented systems that pair a language model with a deterministic calculation layer so that generated figures remain auditable. The constraints in that setting — interpretability, calibration, the cost of a false positive to a real clinician — have shaped how I evaluate models generally.

I am currently looking for full-time machine learning engineer roles and, in parallel, doctoral programs in multimodal representation learning and affective computing. If you work on problems where the interesting structure lies between modalities rather than within one, I would be glad to hear from you.
